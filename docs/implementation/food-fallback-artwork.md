# Food fallback artwork

Food and meal records do not share a dependable category field. Their saved or provider image remains the first choice. When it is absent or fails to load, mobile and web choose a **display-only** illustration from the visible food name. The choice is never written to a food, diary entry, nutrition snapshot, or photo gallery. A saved meal always uses the meal illustration. Unrecognized names use a neutral covered dish, so an unfamiliar branded product is not depicted as a specific ingredient.

The shared classifier lives in `shared/src/foodImages/fallbackGroup.ts`. It handles German accents, English and German names, and common compounds. Specific forms such as sauce, drink, or prepared dish take precedence over ingredient words: “Tomate roh” uses vegetables, while “Tomatensoße” and “Tomate frito” use condiments. This is deliberately conservative visual categorization, not a nutrition or search classification. If the API gains trustworthy food-group metadata, that should take precedence over name inference.

Fifteen illustrations cover vegetables, fruit, bread, grains, dairy, eggs, meat, seafood, legumes, nuts, drinks, sweets, condiments, meals, and unknown food. The [contact sheet](evidence/food-fallback-contact.png) shows the full set. All were generated with the built-in image generation tool, reviewed visually, and exported as transparent 256 px PNGs for Expo and 256 px WebP files for Vite. Mobile assets are in `SparkyFitnessMobile/assets/food-fallbacks/`; web assets are in `SparkyFitnessFrontend/public/images/food-fallbacks/`. The source generation output is held outside the repository; the optimized app assets are the deliverables.

The common generation prompt specified a compact centered generic food-group thumbnail, polished natural food photography, soft studio light, transparent background, readable at 48 px, and no packaging, text, logos, people, or unrelated foods. Group subjects were: tomato/carrot/greens; apple/berries; bread; rice; milk/cheese; eggs; chicken/beef; fish/shrimp; beans/chickpeas; nuts/seeds; water/juice; chocolate/cookie; red sauce; mixed meal bowl; and a covered serving dish for unknown foods. The meal, drink, condiment, and generic subjects use their necessary bowl, vessels, or cover. No image depicts a named provider product or promises its exact ingredients.

Search rows, food/meal libraries, and diary rows show the artwork when needed. Actual photos remain available in the image viewer; fallback art is not clickable as a product photo. The app remains usable offline because the fallback assets are bundled. A broken remote image also resolves to the appropriate bundled illustration.

Validation: shared name-classification cases, web asset URL and search-card cases, mobile thumbnail and terminal-image-failure cases, the related food-search/library regressions, frontend validation and development build, and an iOS Expo export that included all 15 assets. Physical-device appearance still needs review in a subsequent build. Full mobile validation is currently blocked by the pre-existing locale audit and an unrelated unused `GAP_USER_LABEL` export; both also occur outside these changes.
